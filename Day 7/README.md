# Day 7 – Common Mistakes Beginners Make in Data Analytics

## Why Many Analytics Efforts Fail Before Delivering Value

Data analytics failures rarely originate from algorithms or tools.
They most often arise from **incorrect analytical habits, flawed assumptions, and weak reasoning discipline**.

Beginners frequently focus on mechanics — writing code, generating charts, using tools — while overlooking the thinking patterns that determine whether analysis produces meaningful insight.

This chapter demonstrates the most common beginner mistakes using the **NYC 311 Service Requests dataset**, a real-world, operational dataset representative of governance and service management systems.

## Dataset Context

**Dataset:** NYC 311 Service Requests (Kaggle)
**Domain:** Citizen complaints / service requests / operational records

This dataset closely resembles real administrative and governance datasets:

* High-volume transactional records
* Multiple service categories
* Time-dependent behavior
* Data quality imperfections

Such datasets expose reasoning errors more clearly than perfectly curated academic data.

## Objectives of This Chapter

This notebook and analysis aim to illustrate:

* Why analytics must begin with decision questions
* How silent data issues distort results
* Why aggregation choices matter
* How visualizations can mislead without context
* Why simple analytics often outperforms premature complexity

The emphasis is on **analytical thinking**, not programming complexity.

## Notebook Contents

The accompanying Jupyter Notebook covers practical demonstrations of:

✔ Starting analysis without a defined question
✔ Ignoring data types (especially date fields)
✔ Overlooking missing values
✔ Duplicate record distortions
✔ Incorrect aggregation practices
✔ Weak or decorative visualizations
✔ Misinterpretation of category dominance
✔ Premature analytical complexity
✔ Output-focused rather than decision-focused analysis

Each mistake is paired with corrective reasoning.

## Key Lessons

Effective analytics is primarily a discipline of structured thinking.

Reliable analysis requires:

* Explicit decision context
* Early data validation
* Proper handling of time dimensions
* Preservation of analytical granularity
* Interpretation grounded in domain logic

Tools amplify analytical quality — they do not create it.

## Practical Relevance

These mistakes are not limited to beginners.

They frequently appear in:

* Business dashboards
* Governance reporting systems
* Performance monitoring frameworks
* Operational analytics initiatives

Understanding them prevents misleading conclusions and improves decision reliability.

## Closing Thought

Most analytics errors are not computational errors.
They are **reasoning errors**.

Recognizing them early accelerates the transition from mechanical analysis to decision-oriented analytics.
